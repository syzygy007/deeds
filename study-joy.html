The joy of giving.

It is not sentiment. It is one of the most replicated findings about human happiness: the fastest way to feel better is to hand something over.

The Experiment · Science, 2008

In 2008 the journal Science published a simple experiment. Researchers handed people an envelope in the morning containing five or twenty dollars, with one instruction: spend it on yourself, or spend it on someone else, by the end of the day. That evening, the people who spent on others reported measurably higher happiness. The amount made no difference. Five dollars given away outperformed twenty dollars kept.

Five dollars given away outperformed twenty dollars kept.

Volunteers had been reporting the same thing for years. In the late 1980s, Allan Luks surveyed thousands of them and found a pattern so consistent he gave it a name, the helper's high: a physical lift during the act, then a longer calm after it. Research since keeps circling the same territory, linking regular giving with lower stress and higher life satisfaction. The body appears to be on the side of generosity.

There is a catch, and it is a useful one. The effect is strongest when the giving is chosen rather than obligated, personal rather than abstract, and when you can see it land. Which is, in three clauses, the design brief for Deeds. Pick the deed yourself. Do it with your own hands. Then write down how it felt. The second line on every Wall post is not decoration. It is the data.
